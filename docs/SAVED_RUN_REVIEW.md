# Review of the saved README files and negotiation discussions

Reviewed the project/upstream README files and reconciled **28** saved `conversation.json` files against all six comparison manifests on 10 October 2026: 24 comparison runs and four earlier live runs. Each has a local `summary.json`; the original 18-run index is preserved below, followed by ten later runs. Artifacts are Git ignored and remain in place. The upstream snapshot is pinned and unchanged. The latest code changes in this working tree have **not** been tested against a hosted supplier.

The upstream documentation's main lesson is to study the supplier's particular economics and authority, price complete terms with the published cost function, ask for useful documents, and read the replay after every run. The starter Haggler asks for a cost breakdown and uses `total_cost`; the naive baseline anchors low and accepts early. Neither is a trained winning strategy. The data README confirms the synthetic buyer records end 30 September 2026, index histories have different lags, price units vary, and should-cost is an estimate. The brief explains that valid evidence may help, hurt or fail to persuade a supplier.

## What the saved inputs and supplier replies tell us

**Observed across the ten later runs:** P01's two current-input repeats dropped three selected duplicate cards each; its offer-history repeats dropped none, yet averaged 56.81 versus 71.38. P03's two-pair mean changed only 84.03 to 85.08. P02's first pair scored 24.87 versus 6.57. All ten closed valid autonomous deals with no fallback; these small samples do not establish a broadly superior input. The P02 buyer cited 109 days of notice against a 90-day agreement; the supplier said the notice, quality and should-cost claims did not change its calculation. The buyer's lower EUR 369.15 price at 14 days cost more in total than EUR 370.27 at 45 days. See [full input comparison](INPUT_MEMORY_RESULTS.md).

**Hypotheses, not proven supplier rules:** better costed closing across every current alternative may prevent a low-price/high-financing mistake; a supplier's stated freeze may make payment or duration a better trade than another price-only cut. A rejection of one claim is evidence about that exchange, not a known reservation price. The new deterministic offer selection, claim classification and optional reply-aware mode address these hypotheses locally; they have no hosted score yet.

**Repeated evidence is wasting message space.** In the two P01 economics runs and the P03 economics run, the model again selected cards it had already cited. `render_message` removed the duplicates. For example, P01 run 1 economics turn 2 proposed `notice` and `delivery` again, and the rendered outgoing message had no claim sentence. Both its turn 2 and turn 3 messages lost selected evidence this way. P03 turn 2 repeated its quality and should-cost cards and also sent no checked claim. The model input should list only cards that remain usable, while preserving the full citation history separately for the validator.

**Keep the offer history easy to act on.** In P01 economics run 1, the buyer later lowered the exact same November/30-day price from EUR 133.00 to EUR 131.93. The supplier explicitly said lower prices than earlier proposals did not move the negotiation forward. Run 2 also reduced the same November/14-day package from EUR 132.90 to EUR 132.50; the supplier then described its EUR 133.00 offer as its limit. Put prior buyer terms in a compact structured ledger and instruct the model to avoid a lower price on unchanged terms. A lower price can still be sensible when another term creates a real buyer-cost saving; compare full C before treating price as a concession.

**Choose evidence by who it helps.** P01's contract notice discrepancy was repeatedly acknowledged as new information and was followed by concessions. In contrast, the supplier replied that a rising PPI-C241 figure confirmed its own cost argument. Evidence-first also cited the EUR 143.22 alternative quote, and the supplier said it did not change its calculation. A valid claim is not automatically buyer leverage. The supplier's opening assigns LCI-C 30% and PPI-C241 24% of its costs and says both rose over twelve months. Separately, the category team's part-specific table weights those drivers at 32% and 26%, with electricity (PPI-D351) at 6%; that electricity index declined 3.72% over twelve months. Keep the supplier's stated shares distinct from internal category weights. Rank the argument against the supplier's own cost explanation and use a consistent window; an electricity decrease is a limited counterpoint, not evidence that total costs fell.

**The supplier's notes provide different strategies by case.** P01's dossier notes mention that the owner was not following index costs closely and had asked for faster invoice payment in exchange for a discount. P03 notes say the sales director froze price reductions until December 2027 but was open to payment and call-off terms; price changes above 2% require pricing-committee approval. P03's supplier repeated the freeze during the actual dialogue. The model should turn these notes into dated hypotheses and choose a targeted question/trade, while treating them as potentially stale and never as known private floors.

**The economic menu helped, but the inputs still need care.** Across the six new matched runs, economics scored 71.90 mean on P01 and 88.27 on the one P03 run; current scored 49.05 and 73.07. Every deal was autonomous and every tested claim verified. Yet on P03, after the first package was rejected under a stated price freeze, the model continued to include a price cut in every package. It could have focused the next input and offers on the terms the supplier said were flexible. One P03 pair is too small to conclude this would reliably improve scores.

**Ask questions that produce decision-useful information.** The upstream examples recommend asking for a cost breakdown. None of the saved buyer messages asks for one. Add a short, validated option to request it while making a commercially useful offer. The returned spreadsheet's formulas need careful parsing; the upstream reader converts spreadsheets to text and may expose formulas rather than calculated results. Do not treat an unreadable formula as a cost fact.

## Input version status and next evaluation

The earlier proposal led to `offer_history`, a combined input-and-instruction experiment that was **not** promoted. The implementation now also exposes `unused_claims` and `buyer_history` separately, plus independent `reply_aware` adaptation; all remain optional. The following are evaluation targets, not measured gains:

1. Pass only unused claim cards as `available_claim_cards`; keep `previously_used_refs` for audit. This should prevent a selected card from being silently removed after generation.
2. Add a compact per-thread `buyer_offer_history`, normalized as exact negotiated fields plus price, round and supplier response/status. Highlight packages already proposed. Request a monotone price trajectory for identical terms, and require a full-cost reason before exchanging a buyer-favorable term for a higher price.
3. Add a source-linked supplier-priority summary: note, date, confidence, possible negotiating use and a warning if it helps the seller. Prioritize contract discrepancy and cash/approval clues over dumping more raw rows.
4. Pair every candidate with calculated buyer total cost and alternatives; avoid filling all three slots with near-duplicate prices. When the supplier states a price freeze, present realistic payment/duration/call-off choices and ask for the promised cost breakdown or correct approver.

For a future matched hosted comparison, freeze code, common prompt, case dossier, model and safeguards; vary one optional input or adaptation flag at a time. Track score, complete cost breakdown, deal/must-have/fallback outcomes, claim feedback, rounds and latency, including failed attempts. No hosted comparison was run for the latest implementation. Local scripted scores test the API contract and regression behavior, not persuasion quality.
