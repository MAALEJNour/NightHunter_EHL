# NightHunter: how we negotiated better deals

**Two-minute talk · Tacto Negotiation Arena · EHL Grand Finale 2026**

This short version focuses on the practical strategy tweaks. The deck uses four slides and includes a speaker script in each slide's presenter notes.

## 1. Better deals start with better questions

**On slide:** Listen → Value → Trade → Close.

**Speaker notes:** Supplier bots open with a number, but a number alone does not tell us where a deal can move. NightHunter improved by making a few practical changes: understand the supplier, value the whole offer, and trade terms deliberately. Here are the moves we used.

## 2. Find the pressure point before you make the ask

**On slide:** Look for supplier capacity, cash needs and contract timing. In competitive bids, use the rival's exact quote.

**Speaker notes:** We stopped treating every supplier the same. We looked for clues about capacity, cash needs and contract timing in their documents and replies. That helped us make a concrete ask. In one competitive bid, we focused on a supplier with spare capacity and a cash need, then used the rival's exact current price instead of vague pressure.

## 3. A good price is only part of a good deal

**On slide:** Price + payment + delivery + quality = total value. Give good choices, use a verified detail, and move with purpose.

**Speaker notes:** We also stopped judging offers by unit price alone. Payment timing, delivery and quality can change the cost of a deal. So we put forward a few choices with different trade-offs, each still good for us. Then we moved in measured steps, backed key requests with a verified detail and answered supplier questions with specific numbers. When a good final offer arrived, we took it.

## 4. Small changes. Measurable practice gains

**On slide:** Original baseline 46.7/100; best measured practice batch 75.1/100; P05 RFQ hosted scores 92.4 and 79.2; separate 10-case final-style rehearsal closed 10/10 deals with zero errors and zero timeouts.

**Speaker notes:** Across nine single-supplier practice cases, our original system averaged 46.7, and the best measured batch averaged 75.1 out of 100. In two hosted RFQ tests, scores were 92.4 and 79.2. A separate ten-case rehearsal completed every deal with no errors or timeouts. These are practice results, not a promise about new suppliers. The takeaway: listen closely, trade the full package, and know when to close.

## Evidence and wording

- The 46.7 → 75.1 comparison is the original baseline versus the best measured practice batch over nine single-supplier cases ([milestones](../MILESTONES.md)); it is not proof that any single tweak caused the whole gain.
- P05 scores 92.4 and 79.2 are two hosted RFQ practice runs ([milestones](../MILESTONES.md)).
- The 10/10 result is a separate final-style rehearsal, not the competition final.
- The strategies reflect the documented lessons: total deal cost, supplier-specific trades, checked facts, measured concessions and safe closing ([lessons](../LESSONS.md)).
